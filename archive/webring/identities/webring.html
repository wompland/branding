One tile, drawn cell by cell

The WompLand mark is a 16 by 16 tile: an ultraviolet square, a one-cell bevel on its edges, and a bold pixel W. Because it is drawn on a grid, it is only ever shown at whole-number scale: 1x, 2x, 3x, 4x, and never anything in between.

Clear space and minimum size

Keep four cells clear on every side, a quarter of the tile. The lockup is drawn at 1x as its smallest size: the tile at 16 pixels, the horizontal lockup at 93 pixels wide. Below that the W stops reading as a W.

Favicons at true size

The favicon is the tile itself: 16 cells for 16 pixels, so every cell lands on a screen pixel. At 32 pixels it doubles cleanly.

On paper and on the night desk

The tile keeps its colors on any ground, like a sticker. Only the wordmark flips: ink on paper, paper on ink.

Please don't

  • Don't put the W in a ring. The ring lives in the widget and the badge row, never around the mark.

  • Don't scale by fractions or smooth it. 5.5x turns cells into mush. Pick the nearest whole number.

  • Don't round or float the badge. Corners stay square; depth comes from the one-cell bevel.

  • Don't swap member colors. A coral W says blogWomp. Each tile's color is its name.

Every Womp is a badge on the ring

The rule: every Womp gets the same 88 × 31 badge and the same 16 × 16 tile. Only two things change: the app's color and the app's 12 × 12 icon. A new Womp picks a color, draws an icon, and joins the ring with no other design work.

Frame
One ink cell around the edge, then a one-cell bevel: light on the top and left, dark on the bottom and right.
Tile
The member's 16 × 16 tile at cells 6 to 21, the same tile that is the favicon and the mark.
Name
The job in the member color, then "Womp" in paper, set in the badge alphabet: five cells tall, one cell between letters.
Ring line
RING MEMBER in micro caps. WompLand says RING HOST, an empty slot says JOIN THE RING.

At 1x, where badges live

On a real footer, badges sit side by side at their native size. Each one links to its member.

Colors that pass the gate

Ultraviolet for WompLand, hot coral for blogWomp, aqua for wikiWomp, and signal yellow for whatever has focus or is active. The standard is WCAG 2.2 AA, with AAA (7:1) for body text in both modes. Every number below is read from the theme build's contrast report.

Badge art

Tiles and badges are printed in fixed sticker colors that never change with the mode. They are art, never text, so they sit outside the text gate; the ink glyph on each fill is checked below.

Pixels for the sign, plain letters for reading

Pixel letters are for signage: logos, badges, and headings at 32 pixels and up. Anything you read runs in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, a face built so similar letters stay different. Code is the only thing set in mono.

Badge alphabets · drawn, not typed

Bold: 10-cell caps, 2-cell stems. Lockups only.

Tiny: 5-cell x-height. Badge names at 1x.

Micro caps: 5 cells. The badge's ring line.

Display · Pixelify Sans

  4. Regular weight only, at 32 pixels and up. Below that, and in bold, its C and e close up, so smaller headings use Atkinson bold.

Text · Atkinson Hyperlegible Next

Reading size is 18 pixels at a 1.7 line height, held to about 68 characters a line. Il1 and O0 stay distinct, which matters when someone is copying a fediverse handle or a feed address by eye.

16 pixels is the floor, for captions and interface labels. Nothing smaller carries meaning.

  • Post body 18 / 1.7, regular 400
  • Interface 16 / 1.5, bold 700 for controls
  • Headings Atkinson 22 bold, or Pixelify regular at 32 and up

I put my blog back on a webring

By Sample Author · 14 November 2026 · 4 minute read · sample post

For a few years my blog was a cul-de-sac. People arrived from a search result, read one post, and left by the same door. Nothing on the page pointed anywhere else, because one redesign at a time I had removed every link that was not mine.

Last month I joined a small webring of personal sites that write about tools they build for themselves. The whole membership kit was an HTML snippet and an 88 by 31 button. I pasted both into my footer, and for the first time in a long while my site had a next door.

A ring is an agreement to keep your front doors open to each other.

The button turned out to be the fun part. Thirty-one pixels is not much room, so I drew mine on a grid, cell by cell, and learned quickly that a pixel drawn at one and a half times its size is not a pixel any more. The rule I settled on is whole numbers only: one, two or three times, never in between.

What surprised me is that the traffic is not the point. A ring is a few people agreeing to send readers to each other, in a fixed order, for as long as the sites stay up. The links are slow and small and they stay put. If you run a site of your own, find a ring that fits it, or start one with two friends. Here is all the markup mine needed:

<!-- ring widget for your footer -->
<nav aria-label="Tinkerers ring">
  <a href="https://ring.example/prev?from=blog.example">prev</a>
  <a href="https://ring.example/">Tinkerers ring</a>
  <a href="https://ring.example/next?from=blog.example">next</a>
</nav>

Joining a webring

Last edited by sample-editor on 3 November 2026 · revision 12 · View history

On this page

  1. Before you join
  2. Add the widget
  3. Make your button
  4. Leaving a ring

A webring is a list of sites that link to each other in a loop. Each member adds a small widget with links to the previous and next sites, so a reader can walk the whole ring from any page.

Before you join

Read the ring's rules and check that your site fits the theme. Most rings ask that you post now and then and keep the widget visible on every page. See Running a ring for what hosts look for.

Add the widget

Paste the ring's snippet into your footer template, not into a single post, so every page stays a stop. If your site sends Webmentions, the host can confirm your link automatically.

Make your button

Draw an 88 by 31 image and show it at whole-number scale only. The 88x31 buttons page has a blank template and a pixel alphabet.

Leaving a ring

Tell the host before you remove the widget, so your neighbors can link around you. Good manners are collected in Ring etiquette, which nobody has written yet.
